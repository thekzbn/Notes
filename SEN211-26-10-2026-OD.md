## Enginerring Classifications

  

### Functional

|-> what the software actually needs to do i.e the core problem it is/was created to solve

  

### Non-functional

|-> additional abilities and specifications that software might have that are not necessarily required to perform it's crux function. (For instance, a banking app does not need to be secure to function as a banking app. But it sure as hell is nice to have one that is.)

## Requirement Validation

## SOFTWARE PROCESS MODELS (SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT LIFE CYCLE)
Not all problems can be solved using software. Not all problems are software problems.

When a solution to a software problem has been defined, the next step is to find the method of solving said software problem

SPMs tell us that a software solution is made up of stages.

A process is a structured set of activities used for solving a software problem i.e. all the things you do to see how to solve a problem

These structured sets of activities can be seen from different perspectives called models.

therefore: a SPM is a representation of the activities that make a software.

This means that two models can view the software entity from 2 entirely different perspectives.

### An analogy
See models in the light of transport. Imagine yourself wanting to go meet Sandra(40) who is 48m away from you who has sent you a 3:22 long voice note. Don't see models as the bike or the car you could take to see her, but rather see them as the different routes you could take while using the same car to get to your beloved Sandra(40) who is 48m away from you.

### TRADITIONAL SOFTWWARE PROCESS MODEL
|-> these models are methodologies and viewpoints that have existed for almost as long as the concept of software engineering itself.
Some examples include: waterfall model, prototyping and spiral model.

The waterfall model involves a development method where the development is being done in strict/rigid phases. Once a phase is completed, it cannot be gone back to. It's against the model.

The thing is, you can only use this for a program where the problem has already been heavily specified and the solution is EXTREMELY clear.

1. It is not dynamic
2. it is not adaptable
3. it is often time unrealistic

prototyping is majorly used when the requirements of the problem are not so clear.

It involves creating "dumb" or "smaller" versions of the final system to put through validation so as to get/ensure the requirements of the proposed software

1. It is not cost effective
2. it is not time efficient

the spiral method is a Frankensteining of the waterfall and prototyping models. it's flagship feature is the risk reduction for each stage of the software development life cycle.

It involves estimating the cost and risk involved in each stage to solve them before taking the step. It can be involved to long term projects and it makes it easy to terminate a project based on the estimated risk involved. 

Projects that follow the spiral method have a higher tendency of success. The customer is also available in every step.

1. It is expensive

Agile method involves solution being incremental(process is created step by step) and iterative(process gone through multiple times to get to a solution)
